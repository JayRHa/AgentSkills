# Planning Heuristics

## Variety vs. repetition (decision load + cost)

- Breakfast: 1-2 fixed options on rotation. Most people prefer routine here; it cuts cost and prep.
- Lunch: 2-3 options, frequently engineered as planned leftovers from dinner.
- Dinner: 4-5 distinct meals across a week; repeat one as a second-night or freezer meal.
- Snacks: a small fixed menu (e.g., yogurt+fruit, nuts, hummus+veg). Count their calories.

More variety = more single-use ingredients = more cost and waste. Only raise the variety dial when the user explicitly asks.

## Ingredient-overlap strategy (one trip feeds the week)

Pick anchors, then build meals around them:
- 2-3 anchor proteins (e.g., chicken thighs, eggs, canned chickpeas).
- 2-3 anchor carbs (e.g., rice, oats, potatoes).
- 1 shared flavor base reused across dishes (e.g., onion + garlic + tomato; or soy + ginger + sesame).
- A rotating vegetable set, biased to items that keep (carrots, cabbage, frozen peas) plus 1-2 fresh-and-fast items.

The same roasted-vegetable tray, pot of grains, and batch of sauce should appear in 2-3 different meals with different finishing touches.

## Leftover roll-forward patterns

- Double-batch dinners -> next-day lunch.
- Roast extra protein Sunday -> grain bowls + tacos + salad topper midweek.
- Cook a big pot of beans/grains -> portion across breakfasts, lunches, dinners.
- Designate one weekly "flex" slot to clear the fridge or eat out.

## Batch-prep sequencing (keep the kitchen busy)

Order tasks so the slowest, hands-off appliances start first:
1. Preheat oven; start anything roasting (vegetables, protein, potatoes).
2. Start grains/legumes on the stove or in the pressure cooker.
3. While those run: wash/chop produce, make sauces/dressings, portion snacks.
4. Hard-boil eggs / prep breakfasts.
5. Cool everything before sealing (prevents condensation and spoilage), then portion into containers.

Label each container with contents + date.

## Food-safety storage (refrigerator at <=4 C / 40 F)

| Item | Fridge | Freezer |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked grains/pasta | 3-5 days | 1-2 months |
| Cooked poultry/meat | 3-4 days | 2-6 months |
| Cooked beans/legumes | 4-5 days | 1-2 months |
| Soups/stews | 3-4 days | 2-3 months |
| Cut raw vegetables | 2-4 days | (blanch first) |
| Cooked fish | 2-3 days | 1-2 months |
| Hard-boiled eggs (in shell) | up to 7 days | not recommended |
| Fresh leafy salad (dressed) | 1 day | no |
| Homemade sauce/dressing | 4-7 days | varies |

Rules of thumb: don't leave cooked food at room temp >2 hours (>1 hour if >32 C/90 F). Reheat to 74 C/165 F. If prepping for more than ~4 days, freeze the later portions.

## Budget levers

- Cheaper: dried legumes, whole grains in bulk, eggs, frozen veg, cheaper cuts (thighs, mince), seasonal produce, in-season fruit.
- Pricier/faster: pre-cut produce, canned/jarred, rotisserie chicken, ready sauces, out-of-season produce.
- Cut waste: plan exact quantities, use the flex slot, freeze surplus, repurpose stems/peels into stock.
