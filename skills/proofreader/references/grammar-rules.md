# Grammar, Punctuation & Mechanics Reference

Detailed rules for the correctness pass. Apply these directly when the error is unambiguous.

## 1. Agreement

### Subject–verb
- The verb agrees with the grammatical subject, not the nearest noun.
  - Wrong: "The list of items *are* long." Right: "The list of items **is** long."
- Indefinite pronouns *each, either, neither, everyone, anyone, nobody, someone* take singular verbs.
  - "Each of the players **was** ready."
- *None* can be singular or plural by sense; prefer plural for countables: "None of the books **are** missing."
- Compound subjects joined by *and* are plural; joined by *or/nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
  - "Neither the manager nor the employees **were** informed."
- Collective nouns (team, staff, committee): singular in US English, often plural in UK English. Be consistent.

### Pronoun–antecedent
- Pronouns match their antecedent in number and avoid ambiguity.
- Singular *they* is acceptable for a person of unknown/nonbinary gender and increasingly for indefinite antecedents: "Every user should reset **their** password."

## 2. Verb Tense & Mood
- Keep tense consistent within a passage; shift only with a real change in time.
- Subjunctive for hypotheticals contrary to fact: "If I **were** you."
- Avoid needless tense shifts in narration.

## 3. Pronoun Case
- Subject: I, he, she, we, they. Object: me, him, her, us, them.
- Test compounds by dropping the other noun: "between you and **me**" (not "I"); "He gave it to Sam and **me**."
- *Who* = subject; *whom* = object. Test: substitute he/him — if "him" fits, use "whom." Do not insert "whom" to sound formal when "who" is correct.

## 4. Modifiers
- **Dangling modifier**: the modifier must logically attach to the subject.
  - Wrong: "Walking to school, the rain started." Right: "Walking to school, **I** got caught in the rain."
- **Misplaced modifier**: put modifiers next to what they modify.
  - "She **almost** drove the kids to school every day" vs. "She drove the kids to school **almost** every day."
- *Only* is the most commonly misplaced word — position it directly before what it limits.

## 5. Parallelism
- Items in a list or series share the same grammatical form.
  - Wrong: "She likes hiking, to swim, and biking." Right: "She likes hiking, swimming, and biking."
- Correlative conjunctions (not only…but also, either…or, both…and) must bracket parallel structures.

## 6. Punctuation

### Comma
- Separate independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS): "It rained, so we left."
- After introductory phrases/clauses: "After lunch, we resumed."
- Around nonrestrictive (extra) clauses; **not** around restrictive (essential) ones.
  - "My brother, who lives in Berlin, called." (one brother) vs. "The man who called is my brother."
- Serial/Oxford comma: "red, white, and blue." Pick one policy and apply throughout.
- Do **not** join two independent clauses with only a comma (comma splice). Use a period, semicolon, or conjunction.

### Semicolon
- Joins closely related independent clauses: "The plan failed; nobody acted."
- Separates list items that themselves contain commas.

### Colon
- Introduces a list, explanation, or quote after a complete clause: "We need three things: time, money, and focus."
- Do not put a colon directly after a verb or preposition: not "We need: time, money."

### Apostrophe
- Possessive: singular *dog's*, plural *dogs'*, irregular plural *children's*.
- *Its* = possessive; *it's* = it is/it has.
- No apostrophe for plurals of numbers/acronyms in modern style: "1990s," "URLs."

### Hyphens & Dashes
- **Hyphen (-)**: compound modifiers before a noun ("well-known author"), not after ("the author is well known"). Not after -ly adverbs ("highly rated").
- **En dash (–)**: ranges and connections ("pages 10–20," "London–Paris route").
- **Em dash (—)**: parenthetical breaks. Be consistent on spacing (—em— vs. — spaced —).

### Quotation marks (US convention)
- Periods and commas go **inside** the quotes: "like this," she said.
- Colons and semicolons go outside.
- Question/exclamation marks go inside only if they belong to the quote.
- UK convention often places punctuation outside and uses single quotes first — match the variant.

## 7. Commonly Confused Words

| Confused pair | Quick rule |
|---------------|-----------|
| affect / effect | affect = verb (to influence); effect = noun (result) |
| its / it's | it's = it is |
| your / you're | you're = you are |
| their / there / they're | possessive / place / they are |
| then / than | then = time; than = comparison |
| to / too / two | too = also/excess |
| lose / loose | lose = misplace; loose = not tight |
| accept / except | accept = receive; except = exclude |
| advice / advise | advice = noun; advise = verb |
| principal / principle | principal = main/head; principle = rule |
| complement / compliment | complement = complete; compliment = praise |
| fewer / less | fewer = countable; less = uncountable |
| who's / whose | who's = who is |
| e.g. / i.e. | e.g. = for example; i.e. = that is |
| stationary / stationery | stationary = still; stationery = paper |
| discreet / discrete | discreet = tactful; discrete = separate |

## 8. Grammar Myths — Do NOT "Fix" These
These are stylistic, not errors. Leave them unless the author asks otherwise.
- Splitting an infinitive ("to boldly go").
- Ending a sentence with a preposition.
- Starting a sentence with "And," "But," or "So."
- Using "they" as a singular pronoun.
- Beginning with "Because."
