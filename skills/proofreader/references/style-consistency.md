# Style Consistency Reference

Use during the consistency pass. The goal: one consistent choice applied everywhere. When the text already leans toward one valid option, match it rather than imposing a different one.

## 1. English Variant
Pick US, UK, AU, or CA and apply throughout.

| US | UK |
|----|----|
| color, favor, honor | colour, favour, honour |
| organize, analyze | organise, analyse |
| center, theater | centre, theatre |
| traveling, canceled | travelling, cancelled |
| defense, license (noun) | defence, licence (noun) |
| toward, gray | towards, grey |
| -ize (mostly) | -ise / -ize (Oxford uses -ize) |

Also: US uses "period," UK uses "full stop"; date and quotation conventions differ (see below).

## 2. Serial (Oxford) Comma
Decide: include ("a, b, and c") or omit ("a, b and c"). Apply consistently. The Oxford comma prevents some ambiguities, so prefer it when ambiguity is a risk.

## 3. Numbers
Common convention (Chicago-style):
- Spell out zero through nine; use numerals for 10 and above. (AP: spell out one through nine.)
- Always use numerals with units, percentages, money, and measurements: "5 km," "3%," "$10."
- Spell out a number that begins a sentence, or recast the sentence.
- Be consistent within a comparison set: "between 5 and 20" (not "five and 20").
- Use commas or spaces in large numbers consistently: 1,000 or 1 000.

## 4. Dates & Times
- Choose one format: "June 8, 2026" (US) or "8 June 2026" (UK/intl). Avoid all-numeric ambiguous forms (03/04/05).
- Time: pick "3 p.m." / "3 PM" / "15:00" and stay consistent.

## 5. Capitalization
- Title case vs. sentence case for headings — pick one.
- Do not Capitalize Random Words mid-sentence for emphasis (use italics if needed).
- Capitalize proper nouns; lowercase generic terms ("the company" not "the Company" unless defined).

## 6. Hyphenation Consistency
Keep the same compound treatment throughout: email vs. e-mail, website vs. web site, decision-making vs. decision making, login (noun) vs. log in (verb).

## 7. Quotation Style
- US: double quotes outer, single inner; punctuation inside.
- UK: often single outer, double inner; punctuation placement by sense.
- Use straight (") or curly (“”) quotes consistently, not mixed.

## 8. Abbreviations & Acronyms
- Define on first use: "natural language processing (NLP)" then "NLP."
- Be consistent: "e.g." vs. "eg"; "U.S." vs. "US"; "Dr." vs. "Dr."
- Don't start a sentence with a lowercase abbreviation.

## 9. Lists
- Parallel grammatical structure across all bullets.
- Consistent punctuation: either end every bullet with a period or none (full sentences → periods; fragments → usually none).
- Consistent capitalization of the first word.

## 10. Voice & Tense
- Maintain a consistent point of view (first/second/third person).
- Keep tense stable unless the timeline genuinely shifts.

## 11. Terminology
- Use one term per concept ("user" vs. "customer" vs. "client" — pick one).
- Keep product, feature, and proper names spelled exactly as the author/brand defines them.

## Consistency Audit Checklist
- [ ] One English variant throughout
- [ ] Serial comma policy uniform
- [ ] Number style uniform
- [ ] Date/time format uniform
- [ ] Heading case uniform
- [ ] Hyphenation of recurring compounds uniform
- [ ] Quote style and curliness uniform
- [ ] Acronyms defined once, used consistently
- [ ] List punctuation/capitalization uniform
- [ ] One term per concept
