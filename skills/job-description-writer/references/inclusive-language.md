# Inclusive & Bias-Free Language Reference

Use this when drafting and when applying linter output. Goal: widen the qualified pool, avoid exclusionary or legally risky phrasing.

## 1. Gendered / coded terms → neutral
| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| he/she, his/her | they/their, you/your | Gender-neutral |
| manpower, man-hours | workforce, staffing, work hours | Gendered |
| chairman, foreman, salesman | chair, supervisor, salesperson | Gendered titles |
| rockstar, ninja, guru, wizard | skilled, experienced, expert | Coded, deters some applicants |
| aggressive, dominant, competitive | proactive, driven, motivated | Male-coded; can deter women |
| strong, fearless, decisive (overuse) | thoughtful, collaborative, effective | Balance masculine-coded clusters |
| young, energetic, digital native | adaptable, enthusiastic, curious | Age bias / proxy |
| recent graduate (as requirement) | early-career; or state skills | Age bias |

## 2. Requirement inflation → realistic
| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "Bachelor's degree required" (when not essential) | "Equivalent practical experience accepted" or remove |
| "10+ years of X" for a mid role | "Demonstrated experience with X" or a realistic range |
| Long list of named frameworks as must-have | 2-3 core, mark the rest "or similar / nice to have" |
| "Expert in everything" stacks | Separate must-have from nice-to-have |

## 3. Ability / accessibility
- Only include physical requirements (lifting, standing, driving) if they are **bona fide** to the job. Otherwise they may exclude qualified candidates and raise ADA/equivalent concerns.
- Avoid "able-bodied," "must be able to walk/stand all day" unless genuinely required; describe the actual task and offer reasonable accommodation language.
- Add: "We provide reasonable accommodations throughout the hiring process — let us know what you need."

## 4. Tone shifts that improve apply rate
- Use second person: "You'll own..." instead of "The candidate will be responsible for..."
- Frame requirements as an invitation: "You'll bring..." / "It helps if you have..."
- Add an encouragement line: "Research shows people from underrepresented groups often only apply when they meet every requirement. If you're excited about this role but don't match it perfectly, we still want to hear from you."

## 5. EEO statement template
> [Company] is an equal-opportunity employer. We celebrate diversity and are committed to creating an inclusive environment for all employees. We do not discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, or any other protected characteristic.

## 6. Compensation transparency notes
- Include a salary range whenever possible; it increases qualified applications.
- Legally required to post ranges in several US states (CA, CO, NY, WA, etc.) and increasingly under the EU Pay Transparency Directive. When in doubt, include it.
- State currency and period (e.g., "€65,000–€80,000 / year") and what's variable (bonus, equity, commission).
