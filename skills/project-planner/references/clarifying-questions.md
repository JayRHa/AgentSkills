# Clarifying Questions (Step 1)

Ask only what's missing, and at most 3–5 questions. Prefer making a stated assumption over blocking, but never invent a hard deadline or team size.

## The essentials (must know before planning)
1. **Definition of done** — what single deliverable means the project is complete? What does "good enough to ship" look like?
2. **Deadline** — is there a hard date or external commitment? Is the date or the scope the fixed constraint?
3. **Resources** — how many people, which skills/roles, and what % of their time is available?
4. **Fixed external dependencies** — vendors, approvals, other teams, procurement, environments you don't control?
5. **Constraints & non-goals** — budget cap, tech/compliance constraints, and explicitly what is OUT of scope.

## Useful follow-ups (ask if relevant)
- What's the biggest unknown or riskiest part? (Candidate for an early spike.)
- Are there phases/gates that need sign-off before continuing?
- Has anything similar been done before? (Reference-class for estimates.)
- What is the cost of being late vs. the cost of cutting scope? (Drives crash vs. descope decisions.)
- Any blackout periods (holidays, freezes, key-person vacation)?

## How to handle answers
- If the user gives a deadline but no scope flexibility, flag early whether the critical path fits — surface the conflict before detailed planning.
- If resources are "just me", duration ≈ total critical-path effort; near-zero parallelism. Set expectations accordingly.
- Record every assumption you make in the plan's Assumptions section so they're auditable.
