# Quadrant Generation Prompts

Use these prompts to surface non-obvious, high-signal factors. Aim for 4-8 strong items per quadrant. Reject anything that fails the internal/external or present/future test.

## Strengths (Internal, present, advantageous)

Ask: What do we do better than alternatives, and why?

- What capabilities, skills, or expertise do we have that rivals lack?
- What proprietary assets do we own? (IP, patents, data, brand, network effects)
- Where is our cost structure or margin advantaged?
- What do customers consistently praise? (cite NPS, reviews, retention)
- What financial strengths exist? (cash, recurring revenue, low debt)
- What relationships or distribution channels are hard to replicate?
- What have we proven we can execute that others struggle with?

Test: Is it internal AND true today AND a relative advantage? If it's only "we want to be good at X," it's not a strength.

## Weaknesses (Internal, present, disadvantageous)

Ask: Where are we worse than alternatives, and what does it cost us?

- What capabilities or talent are we missing?
- Where do customers complain or churn? (quantify)
- What technical debt, scalability, or quality limits exist?
- Where is our cost structure bloated or our margin thin?
- What single points of failure exist? (key person, one customer, one supplier)
- Where are we slow, under-resourced, or out of focus?
- What can competitors do that we cannot?

Test: Is it internal AND fixable in principle by us? An external trend hurting us is a THREAT, not a weakness.

## Opportunities (External, future-leaning, favorable)

Ask: What external changes could we exploit if we act?

- What market segments are underserved or growing?
- What competitor is weakening, exiting, or mispricing?
- What new technology, platform, or standard could we ride?
- What regulatory, demographic, or behavioral shift opens a door?
- What partnerships, channels, or geographies are newly accessible?
- What unmet customer job is emerging?
- What macro tailwind (cost decline, adoption curve) favors us?

Test: Is it EXTERNAL and a change we could capture? "Increase sales" is a goal, not an opportunity. The opportunity is the external opening that makes increased sales possible.

## Threats (External, future-leaning, unfavorable)

Ask: What external changes could damage us if we do nothing?

- Which competitors are advancing, and how fast?
- What substitutes or new entrants threaten our position?
- What regulatory, legal, or compliance risk is rising?
- What macro headwinds (recession, rates, FX, supply chain) loom?
- What technology shift could obsolete our model?
- What customer or channel concentration risk could turn against us?
- What reputational or platform-dependency risk exists?

Test: Is it EXTERNAL and outside our control? An internal gap is a weakness; the external force that exploits that gap is the threat. Note the time horizon (now / 1-2y / 3-5y) and rough magnitude.

## Symmetry note

Most trends are double-edged. AI, new regulation, or a shifting buyer behavior is an opportunity for the prepared and a threat to the unprepared. When you list a strong O, check whether its mirror belongs in T, and vice versa.
