# Tone Guide

Tone is two dials: **formality** (casual <-> formal) and **warmth** (neutral <-> warm). Set each independently based on relationship, recipient seniority, topic sensitivity, and culture.

## The Dial

| Situation | Formality | Warmth |
|---|---|---|
| Close internal peer, routine | Casual | Warm |
| Internal, cross-team, routine | Neutral | Neutral-warm |
| Senior internal stakeholder | Formal-ish | Neutral-warm |
| External, established | Neutral | Warm |
| External, first contact | Formal | Warm |
| Legal / HR / finance / contracts | Formal | Neutral |
| Bad news / apology | Neutral-formal | Warm |
| Escalation / deadline slipped | Formal | Neutral (firm) |

## Greetings by Formality
- Casual: `Hey Sam,` / `Hi Sam,`
- Neutral: `Hi Sam,` / `Hello Sam,`
- Formal: `Dear Ms. Rivera,` / `Dear Dr. Lee,`
- Group: `Hi team,` / `Hello all,` / `Dear colleagues,`

## Sign-offs by Formality
- Casual: `Thanks,` / `Cheers,` / `Talk soon,`
- Neutral: `Best,` / `Best regards,` / `Thanks so much,`
- Formal: `Kind regards,` / `Sincerely,` / `Respectfully,`
- Avoid: `Rgds`, `Sent from my iPhone` as a sign-off, smiley-only closings in formal mail.

## Phrase Swaps (weak -> strong)
| Weak / passive | Stronger |
|---|---|
| "Just wanted to check in..." | "Following up on <X>:" |
| "Sorry to bother you, but..." | "Quick request:" |
| "I was wondering if maybe you could..." | "Could you <action> by <date>?" |
| "Let me know your thoughts." | "Can you approve by Thu, or flag concerns?" |
| "It would be great if..." | "Please <action>." (when appropriate) |
| "As per my last email" (passive-aggressive) | "Following up on my note from <date>" |
| "ASAP" | "by <day, time>" |
| "To whom it may concern" | "Dear <name/role>" (find the name) |
| "Hope this email finds you well" (filler) | A specific, relevant opener |

## Warming Up a Firm Message
When you must be firm (chasing, escalating, declining), add warmth at the edges so the core stays direct:
- Open with a genuine acknowledgment: "I know you've got a lot on right now."
- Keep the ask itself plain and unhedged.
- Close with a collaborative offer: "Tell me where the blocker is and I'll help."

## Softening Without Going Limp
Use softeners sparingly and only one at a time: "when you have a moment", "if possible", "would you mind". Stacking three makes the ask vanish.

## Bad News
- Lead with empathy, then the facts, then the path forward.
- Don't bury the bad news under five paragraphs of cushion; readers see through it.
- Be specific about what changes and what you'll do next.

## Cross-Cultural Notes
- **Directness varies.** US/NL/Israel tend direct; Japan/UK/India often more indirect and hierarchical. When unsure, err slightly more formal and add one extra courtesy.
- **Titles & names.** In formal cultures, use honorifics and family names until invited otherwise (`Dear Professor Tanaka`).
- **First language.** If the recipient is writing in a second language, favor short sentences and plain words; avoid idioms ("ballpark", "touch base", "circle back").
- **Time zones & dates.** Write dates unambiguously (`Thu, 12 Jun` not `6/12`) and include time zones for deadlines (`5pm CET`).

## Quick Tone Self-Test
Read the draft aloud. If it sounds like something you'd be uncomfortable saying to the person's face — too cold, too groveling, or passive-aggressive — rewrite that line.
