# Worked Example: SaaS Pitch Deck

Shows the full skill workflow: a rough input brief -> finished outline. Use as a model for tone, assertion titles, and speaker-note style.

## Input (what the user gave)

> "Help me build a pitch deck for my startup. We're 'Threadly' — we use AI to turn internal Slack/Teams chatter into searchable, structured company knowledge. Pitching to a seed VC, 10-minute slot. We have 3 design partners and 40% MoM growth in messages indexed."

## Step 1-3 output (anchors + arc)

- **Audience:** Seed VC partner. Sees 50 decks/week. Cares about: market size, why-now, founder insight, traction, defensibility. Fears: thin moat, "feature not a company."
- **Goal / Ask:** Get a follow-up partner meeting (and interest in leading a $2M seed).
- **Core message:** "Company knowledge is dying in chat logs, and Threadly is the first tool that resurrects it automatically — making us the system of record for institutional memory."
- **Constraints:** 10 minutes, ~10-12 slides, confident but not hype-y.
- **Chosen arc:** What is / What could be (pitch).

## Step 4-5 output (slide spine + slides)

### Section 1: Hook & Stakes — emotion: concern

#### Slide 1 — Every company loses 70% of what it knows in chat
- **Idea:** The pain is universal and invisible.
- **On-slide:** "70% of decisions live and die in chat." [faded screenshot of an endless Slack thread]
- **Notes:** "Think about the last big decision your team made. The reasoning, the tradeoffs, the final call — where does it live? Not in a doc. It's buried in a Slack thread nobody will ever find again. Every company is quietly losing the majority of what it knows."
- **Transition:** "And it gets worse the bigger you grow."

#### Slide 2 — The cost compounds: re-asked questions, lost context, slow onboarding
- **Idea:** This pain scales painfully with headcount.
- **On-slide:** Three icons: "asked 5x", "context lost", "onboarding = months".
- **Notes:** "At 10 people you remember everything. At 100, the same questions get asked five times a week, new hires take months to get productive, and the person who knew why you built it that way just left. Knowledge debt compounds."
- **Transition:** "Today, the only fix is manual — and it never happens."

### Section 2: What could be — emotion: hope

#### Slide 3 — What if your chat wrote your knowledge base for you?
- **Idea:** The possibility / vision.
- **On-slide:** "Chat in -> structured knowledge out." [simple arrow diagram]
- **Notes:** "Imagine if every decision, every answer, every 'here's why we do it this way' was automatically captured, structured, and searchable — without anyone writing a doc. That's Threadly."
- **Transition:** "Here's how it works."

#### Slide 4 — Threadly turns conversations into a living knowledge graph
- **Idea:** The product, in one sentence.
- **On-slide:** [product screenshot: a search query returning a synthesized answer with sources]
- **Notes:** "We connect to Slack and Teams, identify the threads that contain real decisions and answers, and use AI to structure them into a searchable knowledge graph. Ask 'why did we pick Postgres?' and get the answer plus the original thread. It runs silently in the background."
- **Transition:** "And people are already relying on it."

### Section 3: Proof — emotion: trust

#### Slide 5 — 3 design partners, 40% month-over-month growth in indexed messages
- **Idea:** Traction is real and accelerating.
- **On-slide:** One line chart trending up and to the right, titled "Messages indexed, +40% MoM".
- **Notes:** "We're live with three design partners. The metric we watch is messages indexed, because it means the product is becoming part of their workflow — and it's growing 40% month over month. They're not trying it; they're depending on it."
- **Transition:** "That stickiness is also our moat."

#### Slide 6 — The more you use it, the harder it is to leave
- **Idea:** Defensibility / why this is a company, not a feature.
- **On-slide:** "Data moat: your knowledge graph is yours and grows daily."
- **Notes:** "Every day, Threadly accumulates a company-specific knowledge graph that didn't exist before and can't be exported to a competitor. The longer a customer uses us, the more valuable and irreplaceable we become. That's the system-of-record dynamic that makes this defensible."
- **Transition:** "And the market for that is enormous."

#### Slide 7 — Every knowledge worker is a user; we start with the 30M on Slack/Teams
- **Idea:** Big market, credible wedge.
- **On-slide:** "Wedge: 30M Slack/Teams teams -> $14B knowledge mgmt market."
- **Notes:** "Our wedge is the tens of millions of teams already on Slack and Teams. The broader knowledge management market is over 14 billion dollars and badly underserved by tools that still require humans to write everything down."
- **Transition:** "We're the team to win it."

### Section 4: Team & Ask — emotion: resolve

#### Slide 8 — We've felt this pain at scale and shipped AI search before
- **Idea:** Founder-market fit.
- **On-slide:** Two founder photos + one credibility line each.
- **Notes:** "I led the internal search team at a 2,000-person company and watched this problem destroy productivity firsthand. My cofounder built and shipped the semantic search that powers it. We've lived this problem and we've built this technology before."
- **Transition:** "Here's what we're raising to win."

#### Slide 9 — Raising $2M to get to 20 paying customers in 12 months
- **Idea:** The ask, concrete.
- **On-slide:** "$2M seed -> 20 paying customers, $500K ARR, 12 months."
- **Notes:** "We're raising a two million dollar seed. It funds two engineers and a go-to-market hire, and gets us from three design partners to twenty paying customers and half a million in ARR within twelve months."
- **Transition:** "So here's the bigger picture."

### Closing

#### Slide 10 — Threadly becomes the system of record for what your company knows
- **Idea:** Restate the core message; land the line.
- **On-slide:** "The system of record for institutional memory."
- **Notes:** "Companies have a system of record for customers, for money, for code. They have nothing for knowledge — the thing that actually makes them good at what they do. Threadly is that system of record. Company knowledge is dying in chat logs, and we're the first ones bringing it back to life."
- **Final line (verbatim):** "Company knowledge is dying in chat logs — and Threadly brings it back to life."

## Call to Action
- **The ask:** "Join us for a 45-minute partner deep-dive next week."
- **Make it easy:** "I'll send three time slots right after this."
