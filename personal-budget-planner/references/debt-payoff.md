# Debt Payoff & Emergency Funds

## Emergency fund first
Before aggressive debt payoff (beyond minimums) or investing:
1. **Starter fund:** ~$1,000-equivalent for small emergencies.
2. **Full fund:** 3–6 months of *essential* expenses (more if income is unstable or single-earner).
3. Keep it **liquid and separate** — a high-yield savings account, not invested in volatile assets.

The fund's job is to stop the next surprise (car repair, medical bill, job loss) from becoming new high-interest debt.

## Two payoff strategies
Always pay **minimums on everything**, then throw extra at one target debt:

### Avalanche (cheapest)
Order debts by **interest rate, highest first**. Mathematically optimal — pays the least total interest. Best when the user is motivated by numbers.

### Snowball (most motivating)
Order debts by **balance, smallest first**. You clear debts faster early, building momentum. Costs a bit more interest but has the best real-world success rate because people stick with it.

| Debt | Balance | APR | Min |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Card A | $600 | 24% | $25 |
| Card B | $3,000 | 19% | $75 |
| Car loan | $8,000 | 6% | $200 |

- **Avalanche** order: Card A (24%) → Card B (19%) → Car (6%).
- **Snowball** order: Card A ($600) → Card B ($3,000) → Car ($8,000).
- (Here both start with Card A — common, and a good sign to start there.)

## The math of extra payments
Every extra unit toward a 24% card is a guaranteed 24% return — better than almost any investment. This is why high-interest debt usually beats investing (after capturing any employer retirement match).

## Order of operations (common framework)
1. Minimums on all debt.
2. Starter emergency fund.
3. Capture any employer retirement match (free money).
4. Pay off high-interest debt (avalanche/snowball).
5. Full 3–6 month emergency fund.
6. Invest for long-term goals.
