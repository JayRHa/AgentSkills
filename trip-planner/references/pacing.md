# Pacing & Sequencing Reference

Good itineraries fail more often from bad pacing than from bad attraction picks. This file holds the rules for sequencing days, handling travel days, and avoiding burnout.

## The core sequencing algorithm
1. **Cluster** all candidate activities into geographic zones (2–4 for a city, or stops for a route).
2. **Lock** fixed-time items (timed tickets, reservations, tours) onto specific days.
3. **Assign** one zone per day, near the locked items.
4. **Order** within the day: open-early/crowd-sensitive sights first, indoor/flexible things midday, sunset/evening things last.
5. **Bookend light** — arrival and departure days run at ~50% capacity.
6. **Insert flex** — at least one half-day of unscheduled time per 4–5 days.

## Anchors-per-day by pace
| Pace | Major anchors | Minor stops | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relaxed | 1 | 0–1 | Long meals, cafe time, naps. Good for families, recovery, slow travel. |
| Balanced | 1 | 2 | Default for most trips. |
| Packed | 2 | 2 | Only for short city breaks or high-energy travelers; not sustainable past ~4 days. |

A **major anchor** = 2–4 hour experience (museum, hike, major sight, cooking class). A **minor stop** = 30–90 min (viewpoint, cafe, small shrine, market browse).

## The realism check
For each day sum: activity durations + transit minutes + meal time (allow ~1h lunch, ~1.5h dinner). If the total exceeds **~10 waking hours**, or there are **3+ cross-city transits**, cut or move something. Walking-heavy days: cap at ~8–10 km on foot unless the traveler is fit and willing.

## Travel / transit days
- A flight under ~3h with airport overhead burns roughly half a day. Long-haul or with connections: treat as a whole day, do nothing demanding.
- Train/bus between cities: count door-to-door time, add 1–2h for stations and getting to lodging.
- Plan the **arrival day** as: get to lodging, easy nearby dinner, a short walk to orient. Nothing ticketed.
- Plan the **departure day** around the departure time minus transfer + check-in buffer; only schedule things that fit cleanly before that cutoff.

## Jet lag
- Crossing 5+ time zones: expect 1 day of adjustment per ~2 time zones, eased by light exposure and staying up to local bedtime.
- Eastward travel (losing time) is usually harder than westward.
- Front-load *low-stakes* activities for the first 1–2 days after a big time change; save reservation-locked highlights for day 2–3.

## Multi-city / road-trip night minimums
| Nights at a stop | What you get |
|---|---|
| 1 | Touch-and-go. Only if directly on the route; you'll mostly see it in transit. |
| 2 | A taste — one full day to explore. |
| 3–4 | Actually experience it, including a day trip. |
| 5+ | A base — slow travel, repeat favorites, day trips. |

Avoid stringing many 1-night stops together — the constant pack/unpack/transfer cycle is exhausting and eats your budget in transfers.

## Family & accessibility adjustments
- **Kids**: shorten anchors, add playground/park time, plan around naps and earlier dinners, keep transit segments short. Drop to relaxed pace minus one stop.
- **Mobility constraints**: verify step-free access and lift availability; prefer fewer, longer stays in one place; pre-book accessible transport.
- **Older travelers**: build in rest blocks and seated activities (boat tours, cafes) between walking.

## Time-of-day heuristics
- **Early morning (open–10am)**: popular crowd-magnet sights, sunrise viewpoints, fresh-produce markets.
- **Midday (11am–3pm)**: indoor/air-conditioned options (museums, galleries) — beats heat and post-lunch crowds elsewhere.
- **Late afternoon**: parks, neighborhoods, shopping, golden-hour photography.
- **Evening**: dinner, night markets, shows, rooftop bars, illuminated landmarks.

## Weather & contingency
- Tag each day's activities as weather-dependent or not.
- Keep an indoor swap ready for every outdoor anchor (and vice versa).
- Don't put the trip's single signature experience on the last possible day — leave a fallback day in case of weather/delays.
