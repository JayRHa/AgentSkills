# Intake Questions — Before Writing a JD

Ask these (batched) before drafting. If the user can't answer some, note assumptions explicitly in the draft and flag them for review.

## 1. The Basics (required)
- **Job title** and **level/seniority** (junior, mid, senior, staff, manager)?
- **Team** the role sits on and **who it reports to**?
- **Employment type**: full-time, part-time, contract, internship?
- **Location & work model**: onsite (where), hybrid (days/week), or fully remote (which regions/time zones)?

## 2. The Outcomes (most important)
- What are the **top 3-5 outcomes** this person owns in their first year?
- What does **success at 90 days** look like? At one year?
- What **problem** does this hire solve that isn't solved today?
- What will they **spend the most time on** week to week?

## 3. Requirements
- What are the **genuinely non-negotiable** skills/experiences (must-haves)?
- What's **nice-to-have** but teachable on the job?
- Are any credentials **legally or functionally required** (license, certification, security clearance)? If not, drop them.
- What **tools / tech stack / systems** will they use?

## 4. Scope & Stakeholders
- Who do they **collaborate with** (teams, functions, external)?
- Do they **manage people**? How many? Direct or matrixed?
- What's the **budget / decision authority** of the role?

## 5. Compensation & Logistics
- **Salary range** (and currency)? Bonus, equity, commission?
- Key **benefits** to highlight (health, PTO, learning budget, parental leave)?
- **Application process** and expected **timeline / stages**?
- Visa **sponsorship** available?

## 6. Company / Team Context
- One-line **company mission** and what the product does?
- What makes this team / role **distinctive** (mission, growth, autonomy, impact)?
- Any **values or culture** signals to emphasize?

## Defaulting Rules
- If level is unknown, infer from outcome scope using the leveling table in SKILL.md and state the assumption.
- If comp is withheld, recommend including a range and note where transparency is legally required (e.g., CA, CO, NY, WA in the US; EU Pay Transparency Directive).
- Never invent specific salary numbers — ask or leave a clearly marked placeholder.
