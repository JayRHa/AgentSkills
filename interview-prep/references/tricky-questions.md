# Handling Tricky Questions

## "What's your greatest weakness?"
Give a **real** weakness + the **system you use to manage it**. Avoid humble-brags ("I'm a perfectionist").
> "I used to under-delegate because I wanted control of quality. I've started writing clear acceptance criteria and doing mid-point check-ins instead of redoing work — it's freed up my time and grown the team."

## "Why are you leaving?"
Frame **toward** something, never **against** the current employer.
> "I've learned a lot here, but I'm looking for more ownership over the full product lifecycle, which this role offers." — Never trash the boss or company.

## Employment gap
One confident sentence, then pivot to value.
> "I took a year for family caregiving and stayed current with two courses and a freelance project. I'm fully focused and excited to be back."

## "Why this company?"
Must be **specific and true** — a product you use, a mission, a recent move. Generic answers ("great culture") signal you didn't research.

## The salary question
1. **Deflect early** if possible: "I'd like to understand the full scope first — what's the budgeted range for the role?"
2. If pressed, **give a researched range** (not a single number) anchored slightly high, based on market data for the role/location.
3. Never disclose your current salary if local law lets you decline; pivot to "my target range is…".

## "Tell me about yourself"
Present → Past → Future, ~60–90 s:
> "I'm currently a [role] focused on [area] (present). Before that I [most relevant prior experience + 1 proof] (past). Now I'm looking to [what this role offers] (future)."

## Curveballs / "I don't know"
For unknown technical or estimation questions: think out loud, state assumptions, reason to a structured answer. The process is scored more than the exact answer. It's fine to say "I'm not certain, here's how I'd find out."
