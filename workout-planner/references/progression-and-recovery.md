# Progression, Deloads & Recovery

## Progressive overload methods
The plan must grow over time. Pick one scheme per exercise:

- **Double progression:** work in a rep range (e.g., 8–12). When you hit the top (12) for all sets, add weight and drop back to the bottom (8). Best general-purpose method.
- **Linear progression:** add a small fixed load each session/week (great for beginners; e.g., +2.5 kg on lower-body lifts). Stalls eventually.
- **Add a set / add reps:** increase weekly volume before load when load can't move.
- **RPE/RIR autoregulation:** train to a target Reps-In-Reserve (e.g., 2 RIR). Push load when the same weight feels easier.

## RPE / RIR quick scale
| RPE | RIR | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0 | Could not do another rep |
| 9 | 1 | One rep left |
| 8 | 2 | Two reps left (good working zone) |
| 7 | 3 | Warm-up / technique weight |

Most hypertrophy work lives at RPE 7–9.

## Deloads
Every **4–8 weeks** (or when performance/sleep/motivation dip), take a deload week: ~50–60% volume or lighter loads. Prevents accumulated fatigue and overuse injury.

## Recovery basics (often the real bottleneck)
- **Sleep 7–9 h** — the single biggest recovery lever.
- **Protein ~1.6–2.2 g/kg bodyweight/day** for muscle goals.
- **48 h** before training the same muscle hard again.
- **Manage total stress** — training adds to life stress; deload when life is heavy.

## Warm-up template (5–10 min)
1. 2–3 min light cardio to raise temperature.
2. Dynamic mobility for the day's patterns (leg swings, arm circles, hip openers).
3. 2–3 ramp-up sets of the first compound, building to working weight.

## When to back off
Sharp or joint pain, dizziness, or form breakdown = stop the set. Persistent pain = see a professional. Soreness (DOMS) is normal; pain is a signal.
