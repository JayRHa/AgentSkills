# Training Splits

Pick the split by **days available** and **recovery capacity**, not by what looks hardcore.

## Full Body (2–3 days/week)
Best for beginners and busy schedules. Each session hits all major patterns.
- Squat/leg, hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push or pull, core.
- 48 h between sessions (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri).
- High frequency per muscle → fast skill and strength gains for novices.

## Upper / Lower (4 days/week)
- **Upper:** chest, back, shoulders, arms.
- **Lower:** quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, core.
- Each muscle trained 2×/week — strong middle ground for intermediates.
- Example: Mon Upper / Tue Lower / Thu Upper / Fri Lower.

## Push / Pull / Legs (3 or 6 days/week)
- **Push:** chest, shoulders, triceps.
- **Pull:** back, rear delts, biceps.
- **Legs:** quads, hams, glutes, calves.
- 6-day version = each muscle 2×/week; high volume, needs good recovery.

## Bro split (1 muscle/day, 5 days)
- Only suitable for advanced lifters with time; low frequency per muscle. Generally inferior to UL/PPL for most people.

## Choosing
| Constraint | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| Beginner, ≤3 days | Full body |
| 4 days, balanced | Upper/Lower |
| 5–6 days, want volume | PPL |
| Limited recovery / older / high stress | Full body or UL, lower volume |

## Cardio placement
- After lifting or on separate days; keep hard intervals away from heavy leg days.
- Fat-loss: 2–4 sessions (mix steady-state + intervals).
- General health: 150 min/week moderate cardio (WHO baseline).
