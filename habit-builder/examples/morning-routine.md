# Worked Example: Building a Morning Routine

## Goal
User wants a consistent morning routine: hydrate, move, and plan the day — instead of grabbing the phone and doomscrolling for 40 minutes.

## Diagnosis
- **Current loop:** Cue (wake, phone on nightstand) → routine (scroll) → reward (novelty/dopamine).
- The phone is the cue *and* the reward. The fix starts with the environment.

## The plan (build good + break bad in one stack)

**1. Break the scroll loop (make the cue invisible):**
- Charge the phone across the room, not on the nightstand.
- Use an old-school alarm clock so the phone isn't the wake-up reason.

**2. Stack the new routine onto waking (make it obvious + easy):**
- "After I turn off my alarm, I will drink the glass of water on my nightstand." (Glass filled the night before — environment primed.)
- "After I drink water, I will do 5 minutes of stretching on the mat I leave unrolled by the bed."
- "After I stretch, I will write my top 3 tasks in the notebook next to the mat."

**3. Make it satisfying:**
- Mark an X on a wall calendar after the 3 steps. The growing streak is the reward.
- Allow the phone *after* the routine — it becomes the reward for finishing, not the cue that derails it.

**4. Start tiny:**
- Week 1 is only: water + 1 stretch + write 1 task. Scale up once it's automatic.

**5. If-then obstacle plan:**
- "If I wake up late and have no time, then I'll just drink the water and write 1 task — never skip the whole chain."

**6. Never miss twice:**
- One chaotic morning is fine. Two in a row → reset that evening: refill the water, unroll the mat, set the alarm clock.

## Weekly review prompt
- Did the chain happen most days? Which step broke first? Was the phone back on the nightstand? Adjust the environment, then keep going.

> For habits tied to anxiety, compulsion, or substances causing real harm, this is not a substitute for professional support.
