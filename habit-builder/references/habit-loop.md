# The Habit Loop & The Four Laws

## The loop
Every habit runs a four-part loop:
**Cue → Craving → Routine (Response) → Reward.**
- **Cue:** the trigger (time, place, preceding action, emotional state, other people).
- **Craving:** the motivation — you want the *change in state* the reward brings.
- **Routine:** the behavior itself.
- **Reward:** the payoff that tells the brain "do that again."

To **build** a habit, make each step work *for* you. To **break** one, break the loop at the cue or reward.

## The Four Laws (build a good habit)
| Law | How |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Make it **obvious** | Habit stacking, implementation intentions ("I will [X] at [time] in [place]"), visible cues |
| 2. Make it **attractive** | Temptation bundling (pair with something you enjoy), join a culture where it's normal |
| 3. Make it **easy** | Shrink to a 2-minute version, reduce friction, prime the environment |
| 4. Make it **satisfying** | Immediate reward, habit tracker, never break the chain |

## Inversion (break a bad habit)
| Inverted law | How |
| --- | --- |
| Make it **invisible** | Remove the cue (hide the phone, delete the app, leave the snacks at the store) |
| Make it **unattractive** | Reframe the costs, highlight what you lose |
| Make it **difficult** | Add friction/steps (log out, use a timer lock, leave the credit card at home) |
| Make it **unsatisfying** | Accountability partner, visible cost, a "habit contract" |

## Habit stacking formula
> "After [current habit], I will [new habit]."

Examples: "After I brush my teeth, I will do 5 squats." "After I sit down for lunch, I will write one line in my journal." The existing habit is the cue — far more reliable than "sometime today."

## Implementation intentions
> "I will [behavior] at [time] in [location]."

People who write a specific when/where are dramatically more likely to follow through than those with only a goal.
