# Structure & Openers

## The 4-block structure
1. **Hook** (2–3 sentences): role + a specific, true reason you're writing, or your single best proof point.
2. **Proof** (1–2 paragraphs): claims of fit, each backed by a concrete, quantified example mapped to a JD requirement.
3. **Bridge** (optional, 1 sentence): address a pivot/gap/relocation, framed forward.
4. **Close** (2 sentences): enthusiasm + availability + soft call to action + thanks.

## Strong openers (steal the pattern, not the words)
- *Proof-led:* "Last year I cut a SaaS company's onboarding time 40%. I'd like to do the same for [Company]'s new self-serve tier."
- *Mission-led:* "I've recommended [Product] to three friends before ever seeing this opening — building tools that [specific value] is exactly the work I want to do."
- *Referral-led:* "[Name] on your data team suggested I reach out about the Analytics Engineer role."
- *Insight-led:* "Your recent move into [market] creates exactly the kind of 0→1 ambiguity I've spent four years thriving in."

## Weak openers to avoid
- "I am writing to express my interest in the position of…"
- "My name is X and I am a recent graduate of…"
- "To whom it may concern, please find attached my resume…"

## Closers
- "I'd welcome the chance to discuss how my [skill] maps to your [goal]. I'm available [timeframe] and can be reached at [contact]. Thank you for your consideration."

## Addressing gaps & pivots (forward-framing)
- Gap: "After taking a year to care for family, I'm returning with renewed focus and have kept current by [course/project]."
- Pivot: "Five years of teaching trained me to turn complex ideas into clear, testable learning — the same skill UX research demands."

## DACH (Anschreiben) note
German-language formal applications expect: sender block, recipient block, place + date, a **Betreff** (subject line) naming the position, formal salutation ("Sehr geehrte Frau/Herr …"), and a handwritten or typed signature. Tone is more formal than US letters.
