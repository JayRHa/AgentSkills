# Worked Example: Career-Change Cover Letter

## Context
Candidate: high-school teacher (6 years) applying for an entry **UX Researcher** role at a learning-tech startup. No paid UX title yet, but ran a self-directed research project and a bootcamp.

## The letter

> Dear Maya Chen,
>
> I've spent six years figuring out *why* students get stuck — not just that they do — and redesigning lessons around the answer. That instinct is why I'm applying for the UX Researcher role at Lumea: turning confused users into confident ones is the same job, with better tools.
>
> In the classroom I ran what was effectively continuous usability testing. When 60% of my students failed the same problem type, I interviewed a sample, found the misconception, and redesigned the unit — lifting pass rates from 40% to 82% the next term. For my UX bootcamp capstone I formalized that loop: I recruited 12 participants, ran moderated usability sessions on a budgeting app, and delivered an affinity map and 8 prioritized findings that the team shipped against.
>
> I'm changing fields deliberately, not randomly. Education taught me to interview without leading, to separate what people say from what they do, and to communicate findings to people who weren't in the room — the core of research practice. Lumea's mission to make learning measurable is one I've lived from the other side.
>
> I'd love to discuss how a researcher who's spent years inside the classroom could sharpen Lumea's product decisions. I'm available immediately and can be reached at alex@email.com. Thank you for your time.
>
> Best regards,
> Alex Rivera

## Why it works
- **Hook** connects a real teaching instinct to the exact role.
- **Proof** gives two concrete, quantified research-shaped achievements — one from teaching, one from the bootcamp.
- **Bridge** frames the pivot as deliberate and names transferable skills.
- Under 300 words, specific to the company, zero apology.
