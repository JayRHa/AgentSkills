# CEFR Levels (quick reference)

The Common European Framework of Reference describes language ability in six levels. Use it to place a learner and set realistic goals.

| Level | Name | Can do | Rough vocab |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1** | Beginner | Introduce self, simple phrases, concrete needs, very slow clear speech | ~500 words |
| **A2** | Elementary | Routine tasks, simple exchanges on familiar topics, short descriptions | ~1,000–1,500 |
| **B1** | Intermediate | Handle most travel situations, describe experiences, give opinions simply | ~2,000–3,000 |
| **B2** | Upper-int. | Fluent enough for regular interaction, technical discussion in own field, clear detailed text | ~4,000 |
| **C1** | Advanced | Flexible, effective use; complex topics; implicit meaning; rare word-searching | ~8,000 |
| **C2** | Mastery | Near-native ease; nuance, idiom, register across virtually any topic | ~16,000 |

## Placing a learner quickly
- **Can't form a sentence?** A0–A1 → maximum scaffolding.
- **Can describe their day in simple sentences, some past tense?** A2.
- **Can give opinions and handle a conversation with errors?** B1.
- **Comfortable but imprecise on abstract topics?** B2.
- **Fluent, hunting for nuance/idiom?** C1+.

## Realistic timelines (guided, ~1h/day)
Rough guidance — varies with language difficulty relative to the learner's native language:
- A1 → A2: ~2–3 months.
- A2 → B1: ~4–6 months.
- B1 → B2: ~6–12 months.
Languages distant from the learner's own (e.g., Mandarin for an English speaker) take longer than close ones (Spanish for an English speaker).

## Using the level
Always teach **one notch above** the current level — comprehensible but stretching. Re-place the learner every few weeks as they improve.
