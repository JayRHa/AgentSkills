# Correction Techniques & Study Plan

## How to correct without killing momentum
The goal is improvement *and* continued speaking. Over-correction makes learners freeze.

- **Recast (default):** repeat what they said with the error fixed, naturally. Learner: "Yesterday I go to store." Tutor: "Ah, yesterday you *went* to the store — what did you buy?" Models the form without stopping the flow.
- **Batch correction:** let them finish the turn/story, then highlight 2–3 key fixes — not every slip.
- **Prompt self-correction:** for errors they likely know, signal gently ("the tense?") and let them fix it — deeper learning than being told.
- **Prioritize meaning-blocking errors** over minor ones (a wrong gender article matters less than a wrong verb that flips the meaning).
- **Explain the pattern, not just the instance:** when a mistake reveals a rule gap worth teaching now, give the rule briefly with one example.

## What NOT to correct (early levels)
- Every article/gender slip.
- Accent/pronunciation perfection.
- Minor word-order quirks that don't block meaning.
Fix these later as fluency stabilizes; early on, fluency and confidence come first.

## Turn the learner's errors into the curriculum
Keep a running list of recurring mistakes. Each becomes a mini-lesson and feeds spaced-repetition review — far more effective than generic grammar drills.

## A simple weekly plan (≈1h/day)
| Day | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Mon | New vocab in context (10–15 words) + short dialogue |
| Tue | Conversation practice (target language) |
| Wed | Grammar pattern in context + writing a few sentences |
| Thu | Listening (song/podcast/clip) + comprehension |
| Fri | Conversation, recycling the week's vocab |
| Sat | Spaced-repetition review of all flagged items |
| Sun | Light/fun: a show, reading, or rest |

Pair with a spaced-repetition app (Anki, etc.) for the vocab/error list. Review beats cramming.
