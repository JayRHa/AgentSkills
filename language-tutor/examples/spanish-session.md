# Worked Example: Beginner Spanish Session

## Learner
A1 beginner, native English, goal: order food and make small talk on a trip to Spain in 6 weeks. ~30 min/day.

## Session: "At a café" (scaffolded)

**Tutor:** ¡Hola! Vamos a practicar pedir en un café. (We're going to practice ordering at a café.) I'll be the waiter. I'll keep it simple. ¿Listo? (Ready?)

**Tutor (waiter):** Buenos días. ¿Qué desea? *(= What would you like? "desea" = formal "you want")*

**Learner:** Yo querer un café, por favor.

**Tutor (recast, no interruption):** ¡Muy bien! Casi perfecto — *"Quiero un café, por favor."* "Querer" becomes **"quiero"** for *I want*. Try it?

**Learner:** Quiero un café, por favor.

**Tutor:** ¡Perfecto! ¿Algo más? (Anything else?) Here's a useful phrase: *"¿Tiene…?"* = "Do you have…?" Want to ask for a croissant?

**Learner:** ¿Tiene un croissant?

**Tutor:** ¡Sí! Excelente. The waiter says: *"Son tres euros."* (That's three euros.) You can answer: *"Aquí tiene"* (here you go).

## End-of-turn batch correction (gentle)
> Two things to remember:
> 1. *I want* = **quiero** (not "yo querer"). We'll see more of these verb changes.
> 2. Great instinct adding "por favor" — very natural.
> Everything else was clear and understandable. ¡Muy bien!

## Vocab captured for spaced repetition
`quiero` (I want) · `¿Tiene…?` (do you have…?) · `¿Algo más?` (anything else?) · `Aquí tiene` (here you go) · `la cuenta` (the bill).
