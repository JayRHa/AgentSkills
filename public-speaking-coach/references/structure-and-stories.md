# Structure & Storytelling

## Proven talk structures
Pick one and make every section serve the core message.

- **Problem → Solution → Benefit:** classic for pitches and persuasive talks. Make the audience feel the problem before you solve it.
- **What? → So what? → Now what?:** great for reports and lessons. The fact, why it matters, what to do.
- **Story arc (hero's journey, simplified):** setup → tension/obstacle → turning point → resolution → lesson. Most engaging; works when you have a narrative.
- **Three-point (rule of three):** "Three things I learned…" — memorable, easy to follow, easy to deliver.
- **Chronological / before-after-bridge:** how it was → how it is → how to get there.

## The open (first ~30 seconds)
Skip the throat-clearing. Open with one of:
- A **short story** that drops the audience into a moment.
- A **provocative question** they'll keep answering as you talk.
- A **surprising fact or statistic.**
- A **vivid image** or a bold claim.
Then state (or foreshadow) your core message.

## The close (last ~30 seconds)
- **Call back** to the opening — bookending feels satisfying and reinforces the message.
- Restate the **one thing** to remember.
- End with a **clear final line** (a call to action for persuasion, a wish for a toast). Never trail off with "so, yeah, that's it."

## Storytelling that lands
- **Specific beats general:** "the night our server crashed during the launch demo" > "we had challenges."
- **Show the stakes and the change** — a story needs tension and a turn.
- **One story per point**, tightly told. Cut backstory the audience doesn't need.
- **Make yourself relatable** — a moment of doubt or failure earns trust faster than a highlight reel.

## Signposting (help the ear navigate)
Spoken audiences can't re-read. Use verbal road signs: "There are three reasons… First… Second… Finally…" and transitions that recap before advancing.
