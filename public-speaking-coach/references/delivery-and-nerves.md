# Delivery, Nerves & Q&A

## Voice and body
- **Pace:** slow down — nerves speed you up. Aim ~130–150 words/minute.
- **Pauses:** silence after a key point lands it and reads as confidence. Don't fill gaps with "um."
- **Volume & variety:** project; vary pitch and pace to avoid monotone.
- **Eye contact:** talk to individuals across the room, a few seconds each — not the screen or floor.
- **Stance:** plant your feet, open posture, purposeful gestures. Avoid pacing and fidgeting.

## Writing for the ear (not the page)
- Short sentences. One idea each.
- Concrete words over abstractions and jargon.
- Contractions and natural speech — write how you talk.
- Read every line **aloud**; rewrite anything that trips you.

## Rehearsal
- Practice **standing and out loud**, ideally recorded or to a friend.
- **Time it** and trim to ~90% of the limit (you'll run longer live).
- Internalize **beats and transitions**, not a verbatim script — so a dropped word doesn't derail you.
- Rehearse the **open and close until automatic**; they carry the most weight and are when nerves peak.

## Managing nerves
- **Reframe:** the physical signs of anxiety and excitement are identical — tell yourself "I'm excited," not "I'm scared." It measurably improves performance.
- **Breathe:** slow box-breathing (4-4-4-4) before going on calms the system.
- **Warm up:** vocal warm-ups and a power-pose minute backstage.
- **Prepare, don't memorize:** confidence comes from knowing your material and your first 30 seconds cold.
- **Accept some nerves** — they sharpen you. The goal is to channel adrenaline, not eliminate it.
- **Friendly faces:** find a few warm faces and speak to them early.

## Q&A
- **Anticipate** the 5–10 likeliest (and hardest) questions; pre-write crisp answers.
- **Listen fully, repeat/rephrase** the question (buys time, ensures the room heard it).
- **Answer concisely**, then stop — don't ramble into new vulnerabilities.
- **"I don't know"** is fine: "Great question — I don't have that to hand, but I'll follow up." Never bluff.
- **Bridge** hostile or off-topic questions back to your core message.

> For panic-level or clinical anxiety, these techniques help but aren't a substitute for professional support.
