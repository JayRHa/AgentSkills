# Gift Idea Categories

Brainstorm across all of these before narrowing — the best idea is usually not in the first category you think of.

## 1. Experiences (low clutter, high memory)
Concerts, classes (pottery, cooking, climbing), a day trip, spa, tasting, tickets, a membership, a shared activity you do *together*. Especially good for people who "have everything."

## 2. Hobby upgrades
Take something they already love and upgrade it: a better tool, premium materials, the accessory they've been putting off, a subscription in their interest. Shows you know what they care about.

## 3. Consumables & affordable luxuries
The nice version of something they use up and won't splurge on themselves: specialty coffee/tea, good chocolate, premium skincare, a great bottle, artisan food. Low risk, always welcome.

## 4. Sentimental & personalized
Photo book, custom map/star chart of a meaningful date, engraved item, a letter, a framed memory, a playlist. High impact for close relationships; calibrate carefully for distant ones.

## 5. Solve a daily annoyance
What do they complain about or fumble with every day? Tangled cables, cold coffee, bad commute, sore back. Fixing it is quietly the most appreciated gift type.

## 6. Practical-but-delightful
Useful *and* a small joy: a beautiful everyday object, a quality upgrade of a worn-out essential. Avoid pure utility that reads as a chore.

## 7. "Things they'd never buy themselves"
The slightly-too-indulgent version of something they want but can't justify. This is the sweet spot of gifting.

## Price-tier framing
For each shortlisted idea, tag a tier so the user can match budget:
- **$ (under ~$25):** consumables, a small hobby accessory, a thoughtful book.
- **$$ (~$25–75):** quality upgrades, an experience for one, a nice consumable bundle.
- **$$$ (~$75–200+):** experiences for two, premium hobby gear, personalized/custom pieces.

## Group / split-gift note
For big-ticket items, suggest a group gift among friends/family — gets the recipient something they truly want without overburdening one person.
