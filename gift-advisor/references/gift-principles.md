# Gifting Principles

## Thoughtfulness > price
Research and lived experience agree: recipients value gifts that signal attention and understanding far more than expensive ones. A $15 gift that references something they said weeks ago beats a generic luxury item.

## Match the relationship and occasion
Calibrate **intimacy** and **spend** to the bond:
- The same handwritten-letter-plus-small-token that's perfect from a partner is uncomfortable from a new coworker.
- Don't out-spend the relationship — an expensive gift can create a sense of obligation or imbalance.
- Occasion sets the floor and ceiling: a milestone (big birthday, wedding, anniversary) warrants more than a casual one.

## Givers and receivers value different things
- Givers over-weight the "wow" of unwrapping; receivers value **usefulness and longevity** more.
- Receivers often prefer something on their wishlist over a surprise — don't dismiss "boring" requested gifts.
- Experiences and consumables tend to be remembered more fondly than physical clutter.

## Avoid these traps
- **Self-referential gifts:** buying what *you* like, not what *they* like.
- **Implied criticism:** weight-loss, self-improvement, or cleaning gadgets unless explicitly wanted.
- **Obligation gifts:** so lavish the recipient feels they must reciprocate.
- **Ignoring constraints:** allergies, space, what they already own, dietary or value-based preferences.

## Presentation
- A **handwritten note** explaining *why you chose it* often outshines the object.
- Thoughtful wrapping and good timing add real value at near-zero cost.
- For experiences/intangibles, present a nice card or a small physical token so there's something to open.

## When unsure
A high-quality consumable + a genuine note is a near-universal safe gift. A well-chosen gift card to a place they love (not a generic one) is acceptable when you truly don't have enough information — pair it with a personal note.
