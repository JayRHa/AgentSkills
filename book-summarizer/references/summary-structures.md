# Summary Structures

Pick the structure by genre and the reader's purpose.

## Nonfiction (argument-driven)
1. **Thesis** — the one central claim in a sentence.
2. **Key ideas** — the 3–7 pillars that support the thesis, each with a one-line explanation.
3. **Evidence** — the most memorable study, example, or story per idea (keep concrete ones).
4. **Actionable takeaways** — what to do differently, as steps.
5. **Who it's for / what it's missing** — optional honest framing.

## Nonfiction (narrative/biography/history)
- Subject + central question the book explores.
- The arc/timeline in a few beats.
- The 2–3 insights or themes the author draws out.
- Why it matters / what's contested.

## Fiction
- **Premise** (1–2 lines, spoiler-light by default).
- **Central conflict** and the protagonist's goal.
- **Themes** — what the book is really *about* beneath the plot.
- **Arc** — beginning → turning point → resolution (only if spoilers are welcome).
- Tone/style and who would enjoy it.

## Depth ladder
| Depth | Length | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-liner | 1 sentence | The single core message |
| Gist | 1 paragraph | Quick orientation |
| Executive | ~1 page | Thesis + key ideas + why it matters |
| Takeaways | bullet list | Application |
| Chapter-by-chapter | 2–4 lines/ch | Study / deep recall |

## Faithfulness checklist
- Did I state the author's actual thesis, not my paraphrase of what I assume?
- Are examples/quotes real (or clearly marked as paraphrase)?
- Did I separate "the book says" from "I think"?
- Did I avoid inventing specifics I'm unsure of?
- For fiction: did I respect the reader's spoiler preference?
