# Negotiable Levers (beyond base salary)

Base is the most visible lever, but rarely the only — or the easiest — one to move.

| Lever | When it's easiest to win | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Base salary** | When you're below band midpoint | Compounds into bonus, raises, future offers — most valuable long-term |
| **Signing bonus** | When base is capped by band | One-time; great for closing a gap without breaking the band |
| **Equity / RSUs** | Startups & big tech | Understand vesting, cliff, strike price, current vs. preferred valuation |
| **Annual bonus target** | Sales / leadership | Negotiate the % and whether year-1 is guaranteed/prorated |
| **Start date** | Almost always | Buy rest, or align to capture a bonus you'd forfeit by leaving early |
| **Title / level** | Borderline leveling | Affects future earning and scope more than a few % of base |
| **Remote / location flexibility** | Common now | Has real monetary value (commute, relocation, cost of living) |
| **Vacation / PTO** | Often flexible | Especially where base is rigid |
| **Review timing** | When base is stuck | "Revisit comp at 6 months tied to goals" |
| **Relocation / home-office stipend** | On-site or hybrid moves | Easy yes for the employer |
| **Professional development** | Most places | Conference/training budget, certifications |

## Total-comp math
Always compare offers on **total first-year comp** and **steady-state annual comp** separately:
- First-year: base + prorated bonus + signing + (year-1 vesting).
- Steady-state: base + target bonus + (annual equity vest at current value).

A higher base usually beats a one-time signing bonus over time because base compounds into bonuses, raises, and your next offer's anchor.

## Equity questions to always ask
- Number of shares **and** total shares outstanding (so you can compute %).
- Vesting schedule and cliff (commonly 4 years / 1-year cliff).
- Strike price and current 409A / preferred valuation (for options).
- RSUs vs. options vs. ISOs/NSOs — tax treatment differs a lot.
