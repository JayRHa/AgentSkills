# Market Research & Anchoring

## Where to pull comparables
- **levels.fyi** — strong for tech roles, real total-comp data by company/level.
- **Glassdoor / Payscale / Salary.com** — broad coverage, self-reported.
- **LinkedIn Salary**, government labor stats, and local/industry salary surveys.
- **Your network** — peers in similar roles give the truest signal.
- **Recruiters** — ask the recruiter directly for the band early.

## Build a defensible range
1. Collect 5+ comparables for your **exact role, level, and location** (remote changes this).
2. Find the 25th / 50th / 75th percentile.
3. Set your **target** near the 75th percentile if your fit/leverage is strong, the 50th if average.
4. Set your **walk-away** at the lowest total comp you'd genuinely accept.

## Anchoring rules
- **Let them go first** when you can. "What's the budgeted range for this role?"
- If forced to name a number, give a **range** whose *bottom* equals your real target (people negotiate toward the bottom of your range).
- Anchor **high but defensible** — high enough to leave room, grounded in data so it's not dismissed.
- Anchor on **total comp**, and be explicit which components you're flexible on.

## Leverage inventory
Rank your leverage honestly — it sets how aggressive to be:
- Competing offers (strongest).
- Scarce, in-demand skills.
- Being currently employed (you can walk).
- Internal: documented impact, retention risk.
- Weak leverage: you need the job fast, no alternatives — negotiate, but modestly.

## Reminder
Salary data drifts and is location/level-sensitive. Always re-verify against current sources right before the conversation; don't quote a stale figure as fact.
