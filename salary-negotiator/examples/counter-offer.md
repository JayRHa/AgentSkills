# Worked Example: Counter-Offer

## Context
Offer for a mid-level data analyst, fully remote (US):
- Base **$92,000**, 8% target bonus, no signing bonus, 15 days PTO.

Candidate's research (levels.fyi + Glassdoor + a peer) puts the role at **$88k–$105k base**, median ~$98k. They have one weaker competing offer at $95k base and are currently employed (real leverage).

Target: **$102k base + a signing bonus**.

## The counter-email

> Hi Jordan,
>
> Thank you for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about the analytics team and the problems you're tackling.
>
> Based on my research for this role and level (remote, US), comparable roles are landing around $98–105k base, and I have another offer at $95k. [Company] is my top choice, so I'd love to make this work. Two things would make it an easy yes:
>
> 1. **Base at $102,000** — in line with market for the scope of this role.
> 2. A **$5,000 signing bonus** to bridge the transition.
>
> If there's flexibility on base, I'm flexible on the signing bonus, and vice versa. Happy to hop on a call. Either way, I'm enthusiastic and ready to move quickly.
>
> Best,
> Sam

## Reasoning
- **Enthusiasm first**, so the counter reads as "help me say yes," not "your offer is bad."
- **Base is the primary ask** (it compounds) — anchored at the top third, justified by data + a real competing offer.
- **Signing bonus is the secondary lever** that lets them concede gracefully if base is band-capped.
- **Explicitly flexible** between the two asks → collaborative, gives them a path.
- **Competing offer stated honestly** ($95k) — never inflated, because a bluff that's called destroys trust.

## Likely outcome
A typical result here: base to **$99–100k** plus a **$3–5k signing bonus** — a $7–13k/yr improvement for one well-framed email. Worst realistic case: they hold at $92k and the candidate decides against their pre-set walk-away, not in the heat of the moment.
